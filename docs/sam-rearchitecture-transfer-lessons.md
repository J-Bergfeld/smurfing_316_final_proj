# Re-architecture lessons for the SAM 5.56 baseline

## Decision and baseline

Use commit `d39531d22fa424d7093dbbdca6ae47ef10a6dd7d` as the frozen SAM control. The user reports an official practice score of **5.56** for it. The user also reports **4.19** for pressure commit `9e8a264` and **4.27** for pressure commit `58a7bb5`. Run IDs, timestamps and scorer versions were not supplied. These are separate official runs; their differences do not isolate a feature or prove what a new candidate will score.

At the time of this audit, the local `sam` branch and the `origin/sam` tip both pointed to other commits; `d39531d` is in the history of `origin/sam`. Base any trial explicitly on the full revision above, rather than assuming either branch tip is the 5.56 artifact. No SAM code was changed by this audit.

The best use of about five official tests is to preserve the SAM controller and test small changes where the re-architecture revealed a concrete weakness. The complete pressure policy is not a justified replacement for the 5.56 baseline. A local simulator screen can eliminate obvious failures and reveal mechanisms, but historical calibration was too weak to reliably predict official rankings.

**Later evidence:** The user reports `0519eb0`, which reduces the healthy combat cube-detour radius from 70 m to 60 m relative to `d39531d`, scored **5.19**. Those are separate official runs and do not prove causality. This makes further restrictions on cube collection less compelling; the hazard-specific proposal below is distinct but should not be promoted without evidence that its trigger occurs and does more good than lost cube collection. Subsequent small local screens of shot clearance (three pairs: one −10, two ties) and combat unstuck (four ties; one candidate late) did not justify prioritizing either for an official test.

SAM already runs the integrated practice/squad policy with supply drops off, the positional field and corner duel on, and the tactical planner absent. It already selects weapon-aware combat spacing: approximately 82 m for the aggressive gun case, 120 m for laser and 28 m for lightning. It already watches recently threatening enemies, evades projectiles, and can fire while moving to a selected objective. These are baseline behaviors, not new transfers. The active SAM Dockerfile and behavior should be verified from the exact commit, not inferred from the current branch.

## What transfers from the mapping work

SAM already has a baked 2 m navigation grid with blocked cells, elevations, clearance and ground connectivity, plus pathfinding and its own exposure calculation. The later work is not a complete replacement map: it adds a canopy-aware exposure grid for hold goals, a roof-access graph and roof-climb policy, and fixes particular invalid roof approaches. It does not establish that the underlying ground connectivity is more accurate; connectivity differences remained in the re-architecture.

The most direct mapping experiment is to feed the canopy-aware exposure grid into **SAM's existing positional choices**, behind a flag. Note that at the `d39531d` defaults `select_shelter` is reached only outside the squad branch, while `BOT_LATE_HIDE` is off: changing its exposure lookup alone may have no effect in practice. The active positional field instead scores current hunters' actual sight lines; a static exposure value would be an additional score or tie-breaker, not a replacement for those threat-specific checks. The current grid loader filters hold candidates rather than exposing a general-purpose dense lookup, so that lookup must be added for arbitrary field candidates. Another option is a quiet idle fallback, which changes goals more substantially. First measure activation and whether position changes improve cover without losing firing opportunities. Check the map hash and retain the existing fallback for other maps. Its four-pair pressure hold result is encouraging but too small and policy-dependent to claim a SAM improvement.

The roof-access graph is a larger experiment: SAM would need a consumer that chooses a roof and handles approach, ascent, combat, descent, zone timing and energy. Simply copying the graph or the adjacent-solid fix changes nothing if SAM never calls it. Only spend one of the five official tests on roof behavior if diagnostics show enough practical opportunity and a locally screened, bounded implementation is ready. The small roof-plus-peek screen changed both features at once and cannot establish a roof-only benefit.

As an alternative mapping-first use of test 4 or 5, prefer the isolated exposure-signal experiment above over replacing ground navigation wholesale. Reserve the existing combat field, corner-duel behavior and 82 m gun tuning as the control, and measure initialization time, action latency, movement, cover, firing time and placement. A better geometric approximation is a hypothesis about score, not proof of it.

## Five-test portfolio

Each row is a separate challenger based on the exact SAM control unless a later row explicitly chooses a previously successful candidate. Do not silently combine changes. Shortlist with paired local seeds and targeted scenarios first; use the official result for the champion decision. Tests are adaptive: if the mechanism does not activate, the local screen shows a clear repeatable regression, or a test fails protocol/timing, use that slot for the strongest remaining candidate instead.

1. **Reassess fight-cube safety:** after the 5.19 shorter-detour result, do not spend a slot on a broader restriction without a demonstrated, frequent hazard and evidence that the remaining cube collection is valuable. Record detours, known threats, upgrades and placement.
2. **Combat unstuck guard:** the initial local screen had four ties and one late candidate action. Defer unless diagnostics show frequent false recoveries; a trapped-fighter counterexample remains essential.
3. **Gun clearance to the target surface:** a three-pair local screen had one large loss and two ties. Defer unless diagnostics show that valid shots are commonly withheld; preserve aim and laser logic in any later trial.
4. **Visible missing-NPC invalidation:** only if diagnostics show meaningful ghost pursuit. Otherwise use the bounded non-firing watch-aim change. Record how often the candidate affects a decision and any missed reacquisitions.
5. **Evidence-led follow-up:** use the most promising isolated result to test a refinement or, if two candidates show independent benefit and no obvious conflict, their combination. If earlier candidates do not justify a combination, consider the safe laser-farming hypothesis instead. Preserve the highest official scorer throughout.

The historical reference code and local proxy are sources of hypotheses, not evidence that a port will improve the official score. If source code is copied, preserve applicable license attribution. Keep candidate flags off in the control and make sure each submitted Dockerfile actually enables only its intended change.

## Evidence and limits

- The 12-pair local `9e8a264` versus `58a7bb5` screen averaged **4.25 vs 3.67**, a paired **−0.58**, bootstrap 95% interval **[−1.67, 0]**; ten seeds tied and two favored the older build. One seed drove most of the difference. There were two late/invalid actions in the older build and none in the newer one. See `mech-testing/score-9e8-vs-58a-422500/report.json`.
- On eight local seeds in pressure policy, weapon-aware range averaged **4.375 vs 3.125**, paired **+1.25**, interval **[−0.875, 3.875]**; one +9 seed dominates and the candidate had two late/invalid actions. This does **not** support retesting that change on SAM, which already has weapon-aware range. `mech-testing/pressure-range-field-422512/report.json`.
- Exposure hold, peek and combined roof/peek results are four-pair exploratory screens in `mech-testing/exposure-hold-screen/`, `mech-testing/peek-screen/` and `mech-testing/roof-peek-screen/`. The combined screen cannot isolate roof from peek.
- The initial architecture comparison also changed supply-drop settings and was inconclusive; another four-pair pressure iteration was materially negative. See `mech-testing/reference-architecture-screen/` and `mech-testing/iteration-screen-followup/`. Do not equate reference action agreement with placement.
- Historical local-versus-official calibration and its limits are documented in [official feedback and historical calibration](m5_official_feedback.md). Official submissions use separate private games, so small differences may reflect sampling or benchmark changes.

Before each official submission, freeze the exact candidate, run relevant tests plus the full suite, build the intended Docker image, and run the restricted protocol check. Record commit, enabled flags, benchmark version, run ID, time, score, late/invalid actions and comparison. Do not spend a test on an identical-code repeat unless the platform confirms it is informative.
