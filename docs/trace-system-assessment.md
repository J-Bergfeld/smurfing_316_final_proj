# Local trace system assessment

Assessment date: 2026-11-22. Scope: `tools/run_local_match.py`, `tools/match_telemetry.py`, `tools/diagnose.py`, the panel/snapshot workflow, and the locally retained `mech-testing/baseline-955-424000` data. The latter is Git-ignored and consists of eight squad-proxy games, seeds 424000–424007; the existing untracked `docs/trace-review-955e6bf.md` describes their provenance. Results below describe this sample, not all runs or official matches.

## Verdict

The system is useful for reconstructing what the **subject observed and did**, obtaining simulator-reported lethal causes, and generating testable local hypotheses. Its strongest evidence is the original wire stream plus terminal simulator fields, not its failure labels. It is substantially less capable at explaining *why* a decision happened, whether an alternative would have prevented a death, or whether a candidate will improve official results. There is no measured overall diagnosis accuracy: the repository has no independently adjudicated ground truth for the tags. Assigning a single numeric quality score would conceal that gap.

Eight focused evaluation tests passed in this review. They cover examples of terminal attribution, layout identity, pairing, and registry behavior, but do not measure diagnostic precision or recall. The schema was inspected but not independently validated against the files because `jsonschema` is unavailable in the local Python environment.

## Main weaknesses

1. **The policy's reasoning is invisible.** Full traces contain observations and the effective action returned by the client, but no structured target selection, mode, competing objectives, navigation failure, or score components. The compact sample is roughly once per simulated second and observed mech IDs can include remembered actors. A fire command is not necessarily a shot, and a listed enemy is not necessarily visible or shootable. The `late`/`invalid` flags indicate client counters, but there is no explicit per-tick distinction between a fresh valid reply, a reused/coast action, and a requested action.
2. **Death labels are weak proxies.** Distinct sources in five seconds do not mean simultaneous crossfire; low-HP firing may be optimal; endpoint movement and displacement can flag reversals or combat movement as stalls. Event timestamps are delivery times, not necessarily exact damage times. The terminal window may miss the earlier commitment that caused the death. No reviewed ground truth exists for false positives, missed cases, or avoidability.
3. **Performance evidence is incomplete.** Incoming damage depends on survival duration and exposure; terminal cubes are not wasted resources by themselves. No reliable shot attempts/hits, damage dealt by target, encounter boundaries, exposure/LOS, objective progress, or decision alternatives are captured. The local proxy's relationship to official play is uncertain and historical calibration is poor.
4. **Reproduction and data integrity are uneven.** Frozen panel inputs, hashes and layout fingerprints are strong, but standalone `--trace` opens with `w` and can overwrite evidence; `--events` uses exclusive creation. A crash can leave either file without an end marker, and the JSONL has no per-run metadata, record sequence, integrity checksum or explicit incomplete status. Panel inputs and raw evidence are ignored locally and may disappear; a matching starting layout does not guarantee the same trajectory under wall-clock deadlines. The v1 schema requires some fields without defining their types and does not enforce event-specific payloads.
5. **Instrumentation can change the run.** Serialization and synchronous writes occur in the match loop; timing-sensitive bot search and deadline handling can change trajectories. The isolated benchmark does not determine the effect on game outcomes. A trace is not a simulator checkpoint: scenario replay of observations is open loop and cannot prove a counterfactual match outcome.

## Recommended improvements, in order

1. **Protect and validate evidence.** Make both output paths exclusive by default, use a run ID and explicit start/end/status records, and write a manifest with source/config hashes, simulator identity, bot and opponent identities, seed, tick rate, runtime environment, and file checksums. On failure preserve a marked incomplete artifact. Validate schema, monotonic ticks, expected terminal, scoring consistency, and event continuity automatically. Define field types and event-specific variants in a versioned schema. Acceptance: deliberately truncate, corrupt, duplicate and interrupt fixture runs; each must be rejected or clearly marked incomplete without overwriting an earlier run.
2. **Add opt-in structured decision telemetry.** Record selected objective/target, mode, key candidate scores, path outcome/rejection reason, and action provenance (fresh, invalid, late/fallback) with tick/run IDs; use explicit unknown values. Keep it separate from simulator truth and throttle or buffer it. Add simulator-side shot, hit, damage, death and actor-position events with IDs where available, without calling the RNG-consuming observation method again. Acceptance: reconstruct a few reviewed deaths from linked records and verify counters against engine events; measure cost and outcome drift with tracing off/compact/full in randomized paired repeats.
3. **Validate diagnosis against annotated examples.** Sample deaths across seeds, causes and bots; have reviewers independently label observable phenomena with explicit uncertainty and evidence windows, then adjudicate disagreement. Hold out games to estimate precision and recall with intervals for multi-attacker and stall detectors; vary window and threshold, report unknowns, and distinguish phenomena from avoidable failures. Add focused fixtures for reversals, deliberate cover contact, sequential attackers and dropped terminal events. Do not market a tag as a cause until a controlled intervention supports it.
4. **Measure encounters and alternatives.** Define fight entry/exit, per-enemy damage and overlap, zone pressure, time-normalized exposure, objective opportunities and movement progress with clear denominators. Save 10–20 seconds before death plus earlier encounter context and links to raw ticks. For priority hypotheses, use controlled state scenarios and randomized paired matches with fresh holdout seeds; report uncertainty, timing faults and changes in survival as well as placement.
5. **Improve external calibration and retention.** Retain immutable compressed traces and manifests for selected panels, and record exact official build, benchmark version, time and per-match outcomes when available. Compare more independent builds across several seeds and opponent lineups, repeat same-seed runs to estimate timing noise, and track ranking agreement and out-of-sample error with uncertainty. Until calibrated, treat local outcomes as hypothesis screens and official outcomes as separate evidence.

## Reproduction notes

Run `python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_evaluation.py' -v` and `python3 tools/bench_observer.py mech-testing/baseline-955-424000/424000.trace.jsonl`. The integrity counts above compare, in order, each full trace's `obs.events` plus terminal `events` against every compact `kind=event` payload; continuity compares consecutive subject observation ticks. Tag counts use `tools.diagnose.diagnose` with the recorded subject, default 35% HP and two-source thresholds, selecting compact rows within each stated terminal window. Full traces and results are needed to reproduce these counts; they are currently local ignored artifacts, not archived by this report. The benchmark and audit results are host-specific.
